Baratz and Beberman

Stories from Joseph and Anna Beberman

Find on the treeAll stories

Sources: records (censuses, death records, Find a Grave) and recorded phone calls with Marsha Siegel and Wendy Bessinger on October 16, 2026, plus Wendy Bessinger's phone note shared October 16, 2026. Record facts are cited to the record; family stories are attributed to their teller, and unproven material is marked as such.

Arrival, 1913

Joseph Beberman was born February 15, 1880, in Russia, and Anna, whose maiden name the records give as Krecun (Marsha has also heard it as "Krekon"), was born December 18, 1884, in Russia (per their death records; both rest at Westlawn Cemetery). The 1930 census shows the family arrived in the United States in 1913. They settled in Chicago, and the household the censuses catch over the years holds the whole next generation: Fanny, Leah, Theodore, and, depending on which record you read, William.

The children

Fanny, born about 1912 in Russia, so an infant on the crossing, married Emil A. Jacobson; the family called her Faye. Her son is Edward "Eddie" Jacobson (records; Marsha Siegel, recorded call, October 16, 2026). Leah, who married Peter Baratz, has her own story document. Theodore, called Ted, was born October 15, 1914, and died December 8, 1986.

William versus Theodore: unresolved

The family also remembers a William Beberman, "Uncle Will," a cook for Walgreens who died young, while Marsha was a small child (per Marsha Siegel, recorded call, October 16, 2026). Wendy Bessinger names William among Leah's brothers too, and says she herself may be named for Grandma Leah's brother William (per Wendy Bessinger, recorded call, October 16, 2026). The records complicate it: a 1950 census shows a William Beberman born in 1914 "At Sea," which fits a birth during the family's 1913 to 1914 crossing, but the tree already carries Theodore Joseph Beberman, born October 15, 1914, and two babies in one year is unlikely unless they were twins. William and Theodore may be two brothers, or one man remembered and recorded under two names. William is held out of the tree pending an answer, and Marsha and Barby are the people to ask. Marked unresolved.

Max Beberman: the family's famous mathematician

Wendy's phone note puts it plainly: "Joseph Beberman brother was ??? Brothers son max beberman." The brother's name is the hole in the note; nobody living knows it. But the nephew's story is family canon. Max Beberman (born August 20, 1925, in Brooklyn; died January 24, 1971, in London) directed the University of Illinois Committee on School Mathematics, the UICSM, and was a towering figure of the "New Math" movement; the New York Times carried his obituary on January 26, 1971. Marsha confirmed the family story on her recorded call of October 16, 2026: Max was the mathematician, he was at the University of Illinois, Barby Baratz told the story first,. So: the family story is confirmed by Marsha, and the famous Max fits the note's generation exactly (a nephew of Joseph, born in the 1920s). The identity on paper is still marked unproven: no record yet names the famous Max's father, and until a birth record, obituary, or university file names him, the link rests on family testimony. Barby, the story's source, knows the whole of it and is the family's living authority.
