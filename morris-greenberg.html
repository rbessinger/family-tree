Siegel and Greenberg

The story of Morris Greenberg and his two families

Find on the treeAll stories

Sources: England and U.S. census records, Cook County marriage and death records, and family testimony from a recorded phone call with his granddaughter Gloria "Gitty" Siegel on October 16, 2026.

Romania, then a London locksmith shop

Morris Greenberg was born April 15, 1879, in Romania, the son of Alter Greenberg (death record). By the spring of 1901 he was in London, living in St George in the East with his wife Yetta, both recorded as Romanian subjects; the 1901 England census gives his trade as locksmith. Their daughter Mildred "Minnie" was born in London on July 5, 1901, and their son Albert "Abe" followed on December 27, 1902 (or 1903; the records differ by a year). The family crossed to America about 1905.

The first family, and Yetta's early death

In the 1910 census in Chicago's Ward 10, Morris and Yetta appear with Minnie, 8, Abe, 5, and a third child, Tillie, 3, born in Illinois about 1907; Yetta's parents, Israel Unger, 70, born in Romania, and Freeda Unger, 69, born in Poland, lived with them. Tillie is very likely a third child of Morris and Yetta's marriage; she appears in no later family account, and what became of her is one of this family's open questions. Morris's granddaughter Gitty never mentioned her (Gitty Siegel, October 16, 2026); the family is asking Gitty about her before the tree adds her.

A Cook County death record for a Yettie Greenberg, who died March 1, 1912, at 32, born in Romania, buried at Forest Park on March 3, 1912, is a strong match for Yetta, though not a certain one: that record names her parents as Saul Unger and Hannah, while the 1910 census puts her parents Israel and Freeda in Morris's own household. If the match holds, Morris was widowed in the spring of 1912 with three small children.

Esther, and the second family

On October 12, 1912, seven months after that death, Morris married Esther Moskowitz in Cook County (marriage record). Esther was born in 1887 in Piotrkow, Poland, the daughter of Vigdor Moskowitz and Gitel (death record). Family stories once said Morris met Esther on the boat; the records say otherwise, since he had arrived in 1905 with Yetta and the children, and married Esther in Chicago seven years later. Morris and Esther's known child is Anne, born October 19, 1915, who married Sam Siegel. Two infant sons of Morris and Esther, born 1913 and 1916, appear in Illinois death and stillbirth records gathered on a shared family tree; they died before they could be part of anyone's memories.

Esther died September 6, 1945, in Chicago and was buried the next day at Forest Park. Gitty had anchored Esther's death to "when Jack was a baby"; Jack Siegel was born in June 1945, so her memory and the record agree once her stray "1965 or 66" is set aside as a decade slip.

Talman Avenue, and the end

Morris worked as a machinist. By 1950 the widowed Morris, 72, headed the household at 6550 North Talman Avenue, his son-in-law Sam Siegel's house, living with Sam, Anne, Gloria, Jack, and his son Abe (1950 census). He died February 19, 1958, at that address and was buried February 21 at Waldheim Cemetery in the First Roumanian Congregation section, the same congregation section where Jacob Siegel, his son-in-law Sam's father, and Jacob's son Maury also lie.

The wider family

Morris's brothers, per Gitty (October 16, 2026): Frank Greenberg, "Uncle Frankie," who had a daughter Miriam in Cleveland; and Naftuli Greenberg, whose family died in the camps while he survived, immigrated to America, and lived in New York. Esther's brother was Shmuel, called Samuel, Moskowitz. One warning the records themselves teach: a shared family tree elsewhere gives Morris and Esther a daughter "Sarahann" whose mother is named as an Esther Applebaum. That is a different couple, and the family tree does not copy it.
