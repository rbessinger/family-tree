Stories from Samuel Schatzman

Find on the treeAll stories

Sources: family records and the Schatzman / Bessinger research project, October 2026. Samuel's death facts are from his 1919 Cook County death record. The passages about his wife and daughters are marked where they rest on leads rather than proof.

The patriarch we know least

Samuel Schatzman was born in 1864 in Drashna, Russia, a son of Moshe Itzhok Schatzman. He brought his family to Chicago, and his son Ancil grew up to found a grocery wholesale business and anchor the family's American story. Samuel himself barely outlived the crossing generation: he died on November 23, 1919, at his home at 2454 Walton Street, Chicago, and was buried the next day at Jewish Waldheim Cemetery in Forest Park. His death certificate is the anchor record for this whole branch of the family, and it is nearly all we have of him so far.

The open question of his wife

Who Samuel's wife was is an OPEN question, and the story document keeps it that way. His 1919 death certificate names his wife as Ida. The 1910 census shows his wife as "Fennie." A member tree researched by Katharine Williams, whose Fuegi-Williams tree overlaps this family heavily, gives his wife as Eva Ovensky, born about 1862 in Russia, who appears as a widowed head of household in the 1920 census with a daughter Ida, and who died July 18, 1922, in Chicago. Eva may be the same woman as an "Eva Omansky" Ancestry once suggested for Ancil's mother. One woman under three names, or more than one marriage, is not yet resolved; the records have been asked for, and nothing here should be read as settled.

Daughter leads, not yet proven

The same member tree gives Samuel three daughters, and each is a LEAD pending a death record that names Samuel as father. Anna Schatzman married Isaac Itsig Rubenstein, called Ruby, in 1908; she died October 19, 1918, in Chicago, leaving children Marshall, David, and Florence, and she may be the very link that connects Katharine Williams to this family. Rose Schatzman married Meyer Dorne and died November 3, 1949, in Chicago; Samuel's Find a Grave entry independently names a daughter "Rose Dorne," which makes her the strongest of the three leads. Ida Schatzman, born about 1893 in Russia, never married, lived with Eva in 1920, and died February 12, 1949, in Chicago. If the death records confirm them, Samuel's story gains a whole second household.

Notes for the family

Samuel's arrival record, his own parents' story in Drashna, and the fate of a possible daughter named Ella all remain unfound. What is solid: a man from Drashna, a Walton Street home, a Waldheim grave, and a son who built Mid-City Wholesale Grocers.
