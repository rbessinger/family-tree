Baratz and Beberman

Stories from Leah Beberman

Find on the treeAll stories

Sources: records (census and death records) and recorded phone calls with her daughter Marsha Siegel on October 16, 2026, and with Wendy Bessinger on October 16, 2026. Record facts are cited to the record; family stories are attributed to their teller.

Joseph and Anna's daughter

Leah Beberman was the daughter of Joseph Beberman and Anna Krecun, Russian-born immigrants whose family arrived in the United States in 1913 (per the 1930 census). Her records give her birth as December 20, 1912, in Illinois and her death as June 4, 1998 (per her death record and the family knowledge base). In census records she also appears as "Lena," the kind of name shortening that runs all through this family. She married Peter Baratz, the civil engineer, himself an immigrant who had crossed as a nine-year-old named Perce Barban. Their daughters are Marsha, born Fern Marsha Baratz, and Barby Baratz.

Her brothers and sisters

Leah's siblings, as the family and the records together give them: Fanny, called Faye, who married Emil A. Jacobson and whose son Edward "Eddie" Jacobson (records and family testimony agree); Theodore, called Ted; and William, called Will, a cook for Walgreens who died young, when Marsha was a small child (per Marsha Siegel, recorded call, October 16, 2026). Whether William and Theodore were two brothers or one man under two names is an open question, taken up in Joseph and Anna's story document; it is unresolved.

The Wicht and Hyman connections

Leah's family braided into Peter's through his sisters. Peter's younger sister Ida Baratz married Hyman Wicht, so Ida was Leah's sister-in-law. Peter's older sister Bess married a Hyman whose first name is still unknown to the family; family lore credits him with inventing the Venetian blind and making real money, and their son Sonny Hyman and daughter Arlene, now Simone Simon, are Leah's niece and nephew by marriage (family lore via Marsha Siegel, recorded call, October 16, 2026; Ida's obituary in the Chicago Tribune, March 9, 2011, also names Arlene and Simon among the family).

The birth date wrinkle: unresolved

Leah's dates do not sit comfortably together. Her death record and family records say she was born December 20, 1912, in Illinois. But if the Bebermans arrived in 1913, a daughter born in Illinois in December 1912 cannot fit, and the 1930 census ages her at about 14, which points to a birth around 1915 or 1916 in Illinois (per the 1930 census). One of these is wrong; the likeliest check is her Social Security record (the NUMIDENT file), which has not been pulled yet. Her date is carried in the tree as the records give it, and the conflict is marked unresolved here rather than smoothed over.
