Siegel and Greenberg

The story of Jacob Siegel and Miriam "Mary" Siegel

Find on the treeAll stories

Sources: Cook County birth and death records, the 1930 census, and family testimony from a recorded phone call with their granddaughter Gloria "Gitty" Siegel on October 16, 2026.

From Lespezi, Romania

Jacob Siegel was born about 1889 in Lespezi, Romania (his son Sam's birth certificate spells the town "Lesped," and Jacob's death record gives "Lespiz"; Lespezi, in the Iasi region, is the likely place). His parents were Sam Siegel and Fanny Markovitz (death record). He came to Chicago and worked as a furrier, the trade his sons Albert and Samuel would follow him into.

His wife was Miriam, called Mary. Sam's 1913 birth certificate records her as Mary Siegel, 25, born in the same town of Lesped, Romania, as Jacob. Gitty testified that her grandmother Miriam was born a Siegel herself, of an unrelated family (Gitty Siegel, October 16, 2026). That is family testimony, not yet proven: birth indexes often repeat a wife's married name, so the certificate's "Siegel" is consistent with Gitty's account without settling it. Her death, about 1961, has not yet been found in the records.

Five children, in census order

The 1930 census lists Jacob and Mary's children in an order Gitty confirmed from memory exactly (Gitty Siegel, October 16, 2026): Albert "Al" (born about 1911 to 1912, a furrier, who married Lillian Joftus); Samuel Benjamin (born July 18, 1913, who married Anne Greenberg); Henry "Hank" (born December 30, 1914, who married Ethel Lubeznik and moved to California); Morris "Maury" (born June 21, 1919, killed in the Pacific in 1944); and Pearl (born December 21, 1922, who married Abe Machefsky).

Waldheim, and the First Roumanian Congregation

Jacob died September 26, 1941, in Chicago, at about 52, and was buried at Waldheim Cemetery in Forest Park, in the First Roumanian Congregation section. That section became the family's ground: his son Maury was reburied there in 1948 after his death in the war, and Morris Greenberg, the father of Jacob's daughter-in-law Anne, was buried there in 1958. Miriam outlived Jacob by about twenty years, dying about 1961; where she is buried has not yet been confirmed, and finding her record is an open item for the family. She lived long enough to be a Gold Star mother, and her youngest grandchildren grew up in a house where her son Maury's memory was kept.
