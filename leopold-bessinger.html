Stories from Leopold Bessinger

Sources: family records and the Bessinger research project, October 2026. Leopold's own account of his birth and arrival is from his 1937 naturalization petition, read as an image by the project. Edith's parentage is from her 1974 Cook County death record.

A child immigrant from Stuttgart

Leopold Bessinger was born in Stuttgart, Germany, on June 1, and even the year is his to argue with: his Social Security record says 1883, while in his own naturalization petition, sworn in 1937, he stated 1886. What the petition settles is the outline. He arrived in New York in 1887 as a small child, traveling under the name Leopold Bissinger, and he grew up to work as a salesman of knit goods in Chicago, living at 943 East 52nd Street in Hyde Park. He waited a long time to become a citizen, naturalizing on November 18, 1937, fifty years after he landed.

Marriage and family

On February 27, 1913, in Chicago, Leopold married Edith Todd Brown. Edith was a Chicago-born daughter of David Brown and Rose Kohn, Bohemian Jewish families whose Chicago story is still being recovered; she died May 19, 1974, and was buried at Graceland Cemetery. Leopold and Edith had three children: Ralph, born July 15, 1915; and the twins Herbert and Ruth Janet, born February 9, 1920. Leopold died in April 1966 and was buried in Chicago on April 22 of that year.

Notes for the family

The 1883 versus 1886 birth year is recorded both ways and flagged wherever it appears; his petition statement is the more personal source, the Social Security record the earlier claim, and no document yet ends the argument. How a Stuttgart child named Bissinger became a Chicago Bessinger is, so far, the whole story of the surname in America: spelled one way on the ship, another way in the life he built.
