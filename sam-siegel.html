Siegel and Greenberg

The story of Samuel Benjamin Siegel

Find on the treeAll stories

Sources: family testimony from recorded phone calls with his daughter Gloria "Gitty" Siegel and his daughter-in-law Marsha Siegel, both on October 16, 2026, and from his published obituary (Chicago Tribune, September 22, 2009).

A Chicago furrier

Samuel Benjamin Siegel was born July 18, 1913, in Chicago (birth certificate), the second son of Jacob Siegel and Miriam "Mary" Siegel, immigrants from Lespezi, Romania. He grew up in a family of five children: Albert "Al," Sam, Henry "Hank," Morris "Maury," and Pearl, in that order in the 1930 census. On November 17, 1935, in Cook County, he married Anne Greenberg. Their children are Gloria Joy and Jack, born June 25, 1945.

Sam worked his whole life in the fur trade. His own shop, Sam Siegel Furs, ended up at 17 North State Street in Chicago. After the shop, he worked part time at Barth Wind Furs on Dempster in Morton Grove and for another furrier on Michigan Avenue whose name the family no longer remembers (Marsha Siegel, October 16, 2026).

The fur business: two family accounts

The family tells the shop's story two ways, and both are kept here. Marsha Siegel, who knew the shop, said Sam was solo, with no partners; his brother Al cut furs at 17 North State on a different floor, and the two were not formally in business together. She placed the shop's later home at 17 North State and thought an earlier location in the Pittsfield Building was possible but was not sure. Gitty Siegel remembered it differently: she said Sam and Al were partners for a time, and that the Pittsfield Building location was "very possible." Wendy Bessinger also believed Sam had partners. The disagreement is recorded as the family holds it; nobody's version has been settled by a document.

The Tallman house

The Siegel family's anchor address was 6550 North Talman Avenue near Devon, the house both Gitty and Jack married from. Earlier homes, per Gitty (October 16, 2026), were around 12th Place and Douglas Boulevard, about 3115 West 15th Street (where Jack was born), and near Congress and Central. The Talman house was a multigenerational one: in the 1950 census, Anne's widowed father, Morris Greenberg, headed a household there that included Sam, Anne, Gloria, Jack, and Anne's half brother Albert "Abe" Greenberg (census record). Morris Greenberg died at that address in 1958 (death record).

The long life, and the family's heart

Sam died in September 2009 at 96. His obituary (Chicago Tribune, September 22, 2009) gives the funeral at Weinstein & Piser in Wilmette and burial at Westlawn Cemetery in Norridge, and names his grandchildren Monte (Cindy), Eric (Tina), Wendy (Paul), and Jennifer (Rich), and great-grandchildren Kailey, Cole, Ross, Carly, and Justin. One small conflict sits in his dates: the Social Security Death Index gives his death as September 21, 2009, while the family's own book records September 20. The tree carries the official record with the family book's date noted.
