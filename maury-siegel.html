Siegel and Greenberg

The story of Morris "Maury" Siegel

Find on the treeAll stories

Sources: U.S. military and headstone records, and family testimony from a recorded phone call with his sister Gloria "Gitty" Siegel on October 16, 2026.

The brother who did not come home

Morris Siegel, called Maury in the family, was born June 21, 1919, in Chicago, the fourth child of Jacob Siegel and Miriam "Mary" Siegel, between Henry "Hank" and Pearl in the family's 1930 census order. His little sister Gitty remembers him playing with her when she was small, before he left for the war (Gitty Siegel, October 16, 2026). He never married.

Service and death

Maury enlisted on October 23, 1942 (enlistment record, serial 36615896) and served as a Private First Class in Company E, 105th Infantry Regiment, 27th Infantry Division. He was killed on July 9, 1944, in the Pacific. The headstone application does not name the island where he died. His regiment fought on Saipan, and the Battle of Saipan ended on July 9, 1944, the day he died, so he almost certainly died on Saipan; that last step is an inference, labeled as one, not a documented fact.

The headstone his brother signed

After the war, Maury's remains were reburied at Waldheim Cemetery in Forest Park, in the First Roumanian Congregation section, the same section where his father Jacob lies, and where Morris Greenberg, his brother Sam's father-in-law, lies as well. His oldest brother Albert signed the application for his government headstone on July 7, 1948, giving his own address as 1427 West Farwell in Chicago (headstone application card). That signed card is also the document that proves Albert existed under his own name, untangling years of census entries that called him Alex or Abe.

A Gold Star mother, a flag, and a photograph

Maury's mother Miriam became a Gold Star mother. In the family's home his loss was the defining grief of that generation; his sister Gitty spoke of him with pride (Gitty Siegel, October 16, 2026). He is one of two young men in this family who died in service in the Second World War; the other is Harold Kollenberg, a cousin by marriage on the Bessinger side, killed three weeks after Maury, in July 1944.
